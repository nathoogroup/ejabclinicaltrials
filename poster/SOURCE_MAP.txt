4. Regularity conditions
   Updated source: PAPER/response_to_reviewers/cache/revision_work_plan.tex,
   lines 58-99, contains the weakened piecewise R2 and R1' with its proof.
   R1' requires Pr(p_n <= delta | H0) -> C*delta for every fixed
   0 < delta <= U_p, with 0 < U_p <= 1 and 0 < C <= 1/U_p.
   R2 uses D_(q,n) = -sqrt(n)*p_n*log(p_n) for q = 1 and
   D_(q,n) = sqrt(n)*p_n for integer q >= 2, converging in probability
   to zero under H1. The explicit p_n -> 0 premise is retained from
   Theorem/regularity_conditions.Rmd, lines 305-341.
   The manuscript and main response still state the older R2; the work
   plan is the source for the newer condition. For integer q >= 2,
   the proof uses stochastic ordering Q_chisq(q)(1-p) >=
   Q_chisq(2)(1-p) = -2*log(p), explaining the proposed weakening.
   The factorization n^(1/(2*n^(1/q))) * D_(q,n)^(1-n^(-1/q)) is
   adapted from Theorem/regularity_conditions.Rmd to the piecewise D.
   PAPER/REVISION/SI.tex, Appendix D, supplies the chi-squared quantile bound.

12. Panel A effect-size estimate
   bayes_risk_analysis.Rmd, "Sensitivity analysis: TSBF using estimated
   effect size" (approximately lines 1831-1851), and the
   estimated_omega_tsbf_critical function (approximately lines 1066-1095).
   The poster displays the existing plug-in rule omega_hat = sqrt(X^2/n)
   and its scale substitution tau_hat^2 = (n/q)*omega_hat^2 = X^2/q.
   X^2 is the observed chi-squared statistic, n is sample size, and q is
   degrees of freedom. The manuscript figure caption identifies this as
   Panel A; Panel B uses the prespecified-effect rule. This addition
   explains the plotted calculation without changing any figure values.
   Since X^2 = Q_chisq(q)(1-p), estimated omega is recoverable from the
   same observed p,n,q inputs as eJAB; it is an extra estimation step,
   not additional observed information. The abstract omits the suggested
   extra-information claim for this reason.

18. Abstract comparison patterns and citation check
   User-requested short citation: Velidi and Wei (2025).
   The primary bibliographic record dates the foundational TSBF paper
   to Johnson (2005), doi:10.1111/j.1467-9868.2005.00521.x.
   The plotted nonlocal-prior BFF is Johnson et al. (2023),
   doi:10.1073/pnas.2217331120, not a Johnson (2025) source.
   These verified dates are used provisionally pending clarification.
   Cached chisq_dimensions_risk_grid.csv supports the broad reversal
   from 2x2/3x3 to 5x5 estimated-omega comparisons and the generally
   comparable or worse eJAB results with true omega. Small 3x3 samples
   can also favor estimated-effect TSBF at stronger effects; true-effect
   TSBF advantages are not uniformly slight near weak signals.
   Cached large_sample_empirical_bic_risk_grid.csv supports small
   differences in single-parameter tests and larger gains for eJAB in
   weaker-effect bands in multiparameter tests. At stronger effects,
   both rules have high power and BIC can gain through lower Type I error.
   Cached dense_bayes_risk_grid.csv supports eJAB gains over p < .05
   at larger samples with sufficient signal, with NHST advantages in
   weaker-signal bands and some small-sample regimes.
